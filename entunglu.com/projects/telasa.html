01 — Overview

A streaming platform built for every screen

TelASA is a video streaming service targeting audiences across Smart TV, iOS, and Android. The challenge was to create a consistent yet platform-optimized experience — where the lean-back, remote-driven Smart TV experience and the swipe-forward mobile experience both feel native and intuitive.

I led the end-to-end design across all three platforms, from initial research and content architecture to full UI design, design system, and developer handoff.

"The core challenge: how do you make content discovery feel effortless on a 65-inch TV controlled by a remote, and equally natural on a 6-inch phone with a thumb?"

02 — The Challenge

Multi-platform design without fragmentation

01

Platform Parity

Users expect a consistent brand and content library across all devices — but interaction patterns differ fundamentally between TV remote navigation and touch gestures.

02

Content Discovery

With a large content catalogue, helping users find what to watch next — without overwhelming them — was the primary UX problem to solve.

03

Lean-Back vs. Active

Smart TV is a passive, social viewing context. Mobile is personal, on-the-go. The UI needed to serve both modes without compromise.

03 — Design Process

Research → Architecture → Design → Test

Starting with competitive analysis of major streaming platforms (Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max) and user interviews with target audiences, I mapped content consumption patterns and pain points. Key insights shaped the information architecture: surface trending content early, reduce navigation depth, and make resume-watching frictionless.

Process / Wireframe Screenshots

For Smart TV, I designed a D-pad–first navigation system with large focus states, high-contrast visuals optimized for viewing distance, and a simplified menu structure. Content rows with hero highlights and category rails emerged as the dominant pattern.

Mobile used a bottom-navigation architecture with swipe-forward browsing, portrait-optimized card layouts, and a persistent mini-player for background listening. Shared design tokens kept both platforms visually consistent.

04 — Outcome

A unified experience across every screen

Design System

Built a shared component library covering both TV and mobile — enabling consistent visual language while accommodating platform-specific interaction patterns.

Content Architecture

Restructured the homepage and browse experience to reduce time-to-content, with personalized rows and contextual recommendations.

Developer Handoff

Delivered annotated Figma specs with interaction states, focus behaviors, and responsive rules for all three platforms.

01 — Overview

Making people data accessible and actionable

Enterprise HR platforms accumulate enormous amounts of employee data — but that data often lives in silos, requiring separate logins, exports, and manual cross-referencing. This project redesigned the core employee profile to serve as a single source of truth across HR, management, and operations teams.

I led the design from discovery through to handoff — mapping the current-state information architecture, defining permission logic with the product team, and designing the new profile experience from scratch.

"An employee profile touched by 6 different teams, built over 8 years. The data was all there — the design just made it impossible to find, understand, or act on."

02 — The Challenge

Complexity by accumulation

01

Information Overload

Over 80 data fields scattered across multiple tabs with inconsistent labeling. Users couldn't find what they needed without scrolling through everything.

02

Permission Complexity

Different roles — HR admin, line manager, employee — needed different views of the same profile. The existing system showed everything to everyone, with sensitive fields only hidden via workarounds.

03

No Clear Actions

The profile was read-only for most users. Managers needed to take actions — approve leave, update roles, flag training needs — but had no clear path to do so from the profile view.

03 — Design Process

Audit → Architecture → Design → Validate

Starting with a full audit of the existing profile — cataloguing every field, its source system, its owner, and who needed to see or edit it — I mapped the information architecture with the product manager and HR domain experts. This revealed that 40% of fields were rarely accessed and could be collapsed into secondary views.

IA Audit & Wireframe Screenshots

The new profile architecture grouped information into three zones: Identity (always visible), Role & Organization (manager-relevant), and Personal & Sensitive (HR-only). A sticky action panel on the right surfaced the most common manager actions contextually.

Role-based views were built from a shared component set, with each field tagged by permission level. This meant the UI adapted to the logged-in user's role without requiring separate screens — reducing maintenance overhead and keeping the design system manageable.

04 — Outcome

One profile, every role, clear actions

Role-Adaptive Layout

A single profile view that surfaces the right fields and actions based on who's viewing — no separate screens to maintain per role.

Action-First Design

Common manager actions (approve, update, flag) surfaced directly in the profile context, reducing navigation and decision time.

Information Architecture

Reorganized 80+ fields into logical groups with progressive disclosure, making the profile scannable in seconds rather than minutes.
